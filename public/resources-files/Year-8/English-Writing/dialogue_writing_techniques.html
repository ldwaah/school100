Advanced Dialogue Writing: Techniques and Realism

Year 8 English - Writing 60 minutes Description Skills

Master Realistic Dialogue!

Advanced dialogue writing creates believable, engaging conversations that reveal character, advance plot, and bring stories to life. Today, you'll develop sophisticated techniques for writing dialogue that sounds natural and serves multiple purposes!

Getting Started: Making Dialogue Sound Natural

The best descriptions use the five senses to create vivid pictures in the reader's mind.

Sight (What can you see?)

  • Colours, shapes, sizes
  • Light and shadow
  • Movement

Sound (What can you hear?)

  • Loud or quiet noises
  • Musical sounds, voices, nature
  • Silence can be described too!

Touch (What can you feel?)

  • Textures: rough, smooth, soft, hard
  • Temperature: hot, cold, warm, freezing
  • Physical sensations

Smell (What can you smell?)

  • Pleasant or unpleasant odours
  • Strong or faint scents
  • Familiar smells that remind you of things

Taste (What can you taste?)

  • Sweet, sour, bitter, salty, spicy
  • Fresh or stale
  • Delicious or disgusting
Basic Sentence:

"The beach was nice."

Improved with Senses:

"The golden sand felt warm beneath my feet. Salty sea air filled my lungs as waves crashed rhythmically against the shore. Seagulls cried overhead whilst the sun sparkled on the turquoise water."

Why it's better: It uses sight (golden sand, sparkling water), touch (warm), smell (salty air), and sound (waves crashing, seagulls crying).

Let's Practice: Dialogue Tags and Action Beats

Dialogue tags ("he said", "she whispered") and action beats (actions that accompany speech) help readers follow conversations and understand how characters are speaking.

Simple Tags vs Action Beats:

Tag: "I can't believe it," she said.

Action Beat: "I can't believe it." She dropped the letter onto the table.

Action beats are often more effective - they show what characters are doing while speaking, adding visual detail.

Varying Tags:

While "said" is often invisible and effective, sometimes use other tags for variety: whispered, muttered, exclaimed, asked, replied. But don't overuse fancy tags - "said" works well most of the time.

Dialogue Tag Techniques:
  • Use "said" most of the time - it's invisible
  • Vary with action beats to show movement
  • Avoid overusing fancy tags like "exclaimed", "murmured"
  • Use tags to clarify who's speaking when needed
  • Let dialogue itself show emotion when possible

Going Further: What Dialogue Should Do

Good dialogue serves multiple purposes - it's not just characters talking. Every conversation should advance the story in some way.

Reveal Character:

Dialogue shows who characters are through what they say and how they say it. A character's word choice, tone, and topics reveal their personality, background, and values.

Advance Plot:

Dialogue should move the story forward. Characters reveal information, make decisions, or create conflict through conversation. Avoid dialogue that just fills space.

Create Conflict:

Characters don't always agree. Disagreements, misunderstandings, and arguments create tension and drive the story. Even friendly conversations can have underlying tension.

Dialogue Functions:
  • Reveal character through speech patterns and content
  • Advance the plot with new information
  • Create conflict and tension
  • Show relationships between characters
  • Build atmosphere and setting

Final Challenge: Subtext and What's Not Said

Advanced dialogue often has subtext - what characters really mean versus what they're saying. This creates depth and realism.

Subtext in Dialogue:

Characters don't always say what they mean. They might avoid topics, speak indirectly, or say one thing while meaning another. This creates realistic, layered conversations.

Example: "Are you sure you're okay?" might really mean "I'm worried about you" or "You're acting strange."

Silence and Pauses:

What characters don't say can be as important as what they do say. Pauses, hesitations, and avoided topics reveal emotions and tensions.

Body Language with Dialogue:

Combine dialogue with body language to show what characters really feel. A character might say "I'm fine" while avoiding eye contact or fidgeting.

Advanced Dialogue Techniques:
  • Use subtext to add depth to conversations
  • Show what characters don't say through pauses
  • Combine dialogue with body language
  • Let readers infer meaning from context
  • Create tension through what's left unsaid
