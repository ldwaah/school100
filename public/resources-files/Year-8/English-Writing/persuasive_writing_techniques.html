Exploring Five Senses

The best descriptions use the five senses to create vivid pictures in the reader's mind.

Sight (What can you see?)

  • Colours, shapes, sizes
  • Light and shadow
  • Movement

Sound (What can you hear?)

  • Loud or quiet noises
  • Musical sounds, voices, nature
  • Silence can be described too!

Touch (What can you feel?)

  • Textures: rough, smooth, soft, hard
  • Temperature: hot, cold, warm, freezing
  • Physical sensations

Smell (What can you smell?)

  • Pleasant or unpleasant odours
  • Strong or faint scents
  • Familiar smells that remind you of things

Taste (What can you taste?)

  • Sweet, sour, bitter, salty, spicy
  • Fresh or stale
  • Delicious or disgusting
Basic Sentence:

"The beach was nice."

Improved with Senses:

"The golden sand felt warm beneath my feet. Salty sea air filled my lungs as waves crashed rhythmically against the shore. Seagulls cried overhead whilst the sun sparkled on the turquoise water."

Why it's better: It uses sight (golden sand, sparkling water), touch (warm), smell (salty air), and sound (waves crashing, seagulls crying).

Let's Practice: Show, Don't Tell

Instead of telling readers how something is, show them through actions, details, and descriptions.

Telling (Boring):

"Emma was nervous."

Showing (Interesting):

"Emma's hands trembled as she clutched her notes. Her heart hammered against her ribs, and she wiped sweaty palms on her skirt."

Why it's better: We see Emma's nervousness through physical details rather than being told about it.
How to Show, Not Tell:
  • Describe body language and actions
  • Use facial expressions
  • Include physical reactions
  • Show how characters interact with their surroundings
  • Let readers figure out emotions from clues

Going Further: Powerful Vocabulary

Choose interesting words instead of boring, overused ones.

Instead of "walked":

marched, strolled, crept, wandered, strutted, stumbled, tiptoed, stomped

Instead of "said":

whispered, shouted, muttered, announced, exclaimed, mumbled, yelled, replied

Instead of "nice":

pleasant, delightful, charming, wonderful, lovely, enjoyable, splendid

Instead of "big":

enormous, gigantic, vast, huge, massive, colossal, immense

Final Challenge: Figurative Language

Use similes and metaphors to create interesting comparisons.

Simile (using "like" or "as"):

"The stars twinkled like diamonds in the night sky."

"Her eyes were as blue as the ocean."

Metaphor (saying something IS something else):

"The classroom was a zoo during break time."

"Time is money."
