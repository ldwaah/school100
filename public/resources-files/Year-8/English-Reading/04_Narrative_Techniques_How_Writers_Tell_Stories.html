Getting Started: Point of View

Point of view is the perspective from which a story is told. The choice of point of view affects how readers experience the story and what information they receive.

First Person:

The narrator is a character in the story, using "I" and "me". This creates intimacy and allows readers to experience events through one character's perspective.

Example: "I couldn't believe what I was seeing. My hands trembled as I reached for the door handle."

Third Person Limited:

The narrator is outside the story but focuses on one character's thoughts and feelings. Uses "he", "she", "they".

Example: "Sarah watched the door, wondering if he would come. Her heart raced with anticipation."

Third Person Omniscient:

The narrator knows everything about all characters and can reveal multiple perspectives. This gives readers a broader view of events.

Example: "While Sarah waited anxiously, Tom was already on his way, unaware of her concern."

Key Points About Point of View:
  • First person creates closeness and immediacy
  • Third person limited focuses on one character's experience
  • Third person omniscient provides multiple perspectives
  • The choice of point of view shapes how readers understand the story

Let's Practice: Narrative Structure and Pacing

Writers use different structures and pacing techniques to control how stories unfold:

Linear Structure:

Events happen in chronological order from beginning to end. This is straightforward and easy to follow.

Non-Linear Structure:

Events are presented out of order, using flashbacks or flash-forwards. This creates mystery and allows writers to reveal information strategically.

Pacing:

Writers control the speed of the story through sentence length, detail, and action. Fast pacing uses short sentences and action; slow pacing uses longer descriptions and reflection.

Cliffhangers:

Ending a chapter or section at a moment of tension keeps readers engaged and wanting to continue.

Going Further: Dialogue and Description

Writers use dialogue and description strategically to develop their stories:

Dialogue:

Conversation between characters reveals personality, advances plot, and shows relationships. Good dialogue sounds natural and serves a purpose in the story.

Description:

Descriptive language creates atmosphere, establishes setting, and helps readers visualise scenes. Effective description uses sensory details (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste).

Show, Don't Tell:

Instead of telling readers a character is angry, writers show it through actions, dialogue, and description. This makes stories more engaging and believable.

Top Tips for Analysing Narrative Techniques:
  • Identify the point of view and consider why it was chosen
  • Notice how the story is structured and how this affects your reading
  • Pay attention to pacing - when does the story speed up or slow down?
  • Analyse how dialogue and description work together
  • Consider how all these techniques create the overall effect
