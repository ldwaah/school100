Master Adding and Subtracting!

Addition and subtraction are fundamental maths skills you'll use throughout your life. Today, you'll practise different methods for adding and subtracting numbers, including working with decimals and solving word problems!

Getting Started: Mental Addition Methods

Method 1: Number Bonds

Use number bonds (pairs that make 10, 20, 100, etc.) to make addition easier.

Example: 7 + 5 = ?

Think: 7 + 3 = 10, and 5 = 3 + 2, so 7 + 5 = 10 + 2 = 12

Method 2: Rounding and Adjusting

Round numbers to make them easier, then adjust.

Example: 48 + 27 = ?

Think: 48 + 27 ≈ 50 + 27 = 77, then 77 - 2 = 75

Method 3: Partitioning

Break numbers into tens and ones, add separately, then combine.

Example: 34 + 28 = ?

34 = 30 + 4, 28 = 20 + 8

30 + 20 = 50, 4 + 8 = 12

50 + 12 = 62

Let's Practice: Written Addition

Column Addition Method:

Example: 347 + 285

  347
+ 285
────
  632

Step 1: Add ones: 7 + 5 = 12 (write 2, carry 1)

Step 2: Add tens: 4 + 8 + 1 = 13 (write 3, carry 1)

Step 3: Add hundreds: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6

Top Tips:
  • Line up numbers by place value (ones under ones, tens under tens)
  • Always start from the right (ones column)
  • Don't forget to add any carried numbers
  • Check your answer by doing it backwards or using estimation

Going Further: Mental Subtraction Methods

Method 1: Counting Back

Start from the larger number and count back.

Example: 15 - 7 = ?

Count back: 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, 8 (that's 7 steps back)

Answer: 8

Method 2: Finding the Difference

Count up from the smaller to the larger number.

Example: 15 - 7 = ?

Count up from 7: 7 → 8 → 9 → 10 → 11 → 12 → 13 → 14 → 15

That's 8 steps, so 15 - 7 = 8

Method 3: Partitioning

Break numbers apart and subtract separately.

Example: 45 - 18 = ?

45 - 10 = 35, then 35 - 8 = 27

Final Challenge: Written Subtraction

Column Subtraction Method:

Example: 523 - 287

  523
- 287
────
  236

Step 1: Ones: 3 - 7 (can't do), borrow from tens. 13 - 7 = 6

Step 2: Tens: 1 (after borrowing) - 8 (can't do), borrow from hundreds. 11 - 8 = 3

Step 3: Hundreds: 4 (after borrowing) - 2 = 2
