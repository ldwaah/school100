Writing for Different Purposes

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Audience & Purpose

Match Your Writing to the Task!

Different writing tasks need different styles, tones, and structures. A letter to a friend is very different from a formal report or and exciting story. Today, you'll practise adapting your writing for different purposes and audiences!

Getting Started: Three Main Purposes

1. TO INFORM (Explain or Teach)

Examples: News articles, reports, instructions, textbooks, letters of information

Features:

  • Clear, factual language
  • Organised structure (headings, subheadings)
  • Present tense usually
  • Third person (he, she, they)
  • Technical or specific vocabulary

2. TO PERSUADE (Convince or Argue)

Examples: Adverts, speeches, opinion articles, reviews, persuasive letters

Features:

  • Emotive language
  • Rhetorical questions
  • Strong opinions
  • Direct address (you)
  • AFORREST techniques

3. TO ENTERTAIN (Tell a Story)

Examples: Stories, poems, diary entries, creative descriptions

Features:

  • Descriptive language
  • Dialogue
  • Past tense usually
  • First or third person
  • Varied sentences for effect

Let's Practice: Formal vs Informal

Formal Writing

Used for: Letters to officials, reports, job applications, essays

Features:
  • No slang or contractions (write "do not" not "don't")
  • Third person
  • Polite, respectful tone
  • Proper greetings (Dear Sir/Madam, Yours sincerely)
  • Full sentences, correct grammar
Formal Example:

"I am writing to inform you that the school library will be closed for refurbishment from 5th January. We apologise for any inconvenience this may cause."

Informal Writing

Used for: Letters to friends/family, diary entries, casual emails, texts

Features:
  • Contractions allowed (can't, won't, we're)
  • First person
  • Friendly, chatty tone
  • Casual greetings (Hi, Hey, Dear...)
  • Can use slang and short sentences
Informal Example:

"Hi! Just thought I'd let you know the library's shutting for a few weeks from January. Annoying, right? We'll have to study at mine instead!"

Going Further: Matching Tone to Audience

Audience = who you're writing for. Always consider:

  • their age (child, teenager, adult)
  • their relationship to you (friend, teacher, stranger)
  • their knowledge level (expert or beginner)
  • What they expect from your writing
Same Message, Different Audiences:

To a friend: "Can't believe we've got a test on Friday! Want to revise together?"

To your teacher: "I was wondering if we could arrange a revision session before Friday's test?"

To the head teacher: "I am writing to request additional revision support for the forthcoming examination on Friday."

Final Challenge: Your Writing Tasks

Task 1: Identify Purpose & Formality
For each text type below, state:
a) Its purpose (inform, persuade, or entertain)
b) Whether it should be formal or informal
c) One key feature it should include

1. A story about a haunted house
2. A letter to your local MP about littering
3. And article explaining how smartphones work
4. A diary entry about your birthday
5. A review persuading people to watch a film
Task 2: Formal Letter
Write a formal letter (80-100 words) to your head teacher suggesting a new after-school club and explaining why it would benefit students. Use formal language, proper letter format, and persuasive techniques.
Task 3: Informal Email
Now write and informal email (60-80 words) to a friend describing the same after-school club idea and trying to persuade them to join. Use casual language and a friendly tone.
Task 4: Informative Writing
Write and informative paragraph (70-90 words) explaining to Year 6 students what they can expect when they start secondary school. Be clear, factual, and helpful. Think about your audience - what would they want to know?
Task 5: Narrative Writing
Write the opening of a story (80-100 words) designed to entertain readers aged 11-13. Make it exciting, use descriptive language, and create a hook that makes them want to read more.
Task 6: Same Topic, Three Purposes
Choose ONE topic: mobile phones, school uniform, or homework.

Write THREE short pieces (3-4 sentences each) on your chosen topic:
a) To INFORM (explain facts about it)
b) To PERSUADE (argue for or against it)
c) To ENTERTAIN (tell a funny or interesting story involving it)

Reflection

Reflection:
Which type of writing do you find easiest - informative, persuasive, or entertaining? Which is hardest? Why? Have you ever written something and had to change your tone for a different audience? How did you adapt it?
