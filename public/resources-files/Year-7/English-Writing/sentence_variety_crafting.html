Sentence Variety: Crafting Interesting Sentences

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Sentence Structure

Mix Up Your Sentences!

Writing with varied sentences makes your work more engaging and sophisticated. This lesson will help you understand about different sentence types, lengths, and structures to make your writing flow beautifully and keep readers interested!

Getting Started: Four Sentence Types

1. Simple Sentence

One main idea with a subject and verb.

Example: "The dog barked."

Example: "My sister loves football and plays every weekend."

Effect: Clear, direct, punchy. Good for emphasis.

2. Compound Sentence

Two main ideas joined by a conjunction (and, but, or, so, yet).

Example: "The rain stopped and the sun came out."

Example: "I wanted to go out, but I had too much homework."

Effect: Shows connection between equally important ideas.

3. Complex Sentence

One main idea and one or more subordinate clauses (parts that add extra information but can't stand alone).

Example: "When the bell rang, everyone rushed outside."

Example: "Although it was late, we decided to watch one more episode."

Subordinating conjunctions: when, because, although, if, since, unless, whilst, after, before

Effect: Adds detail and sophistication.

4. Compound-Complex Sentence

Combines compound and complex - multiple main ideas AND subordinate clauses.

Example: "When the storm hit, we ran inside, but we were already soaked."

Effect: Very sophisticated, shows multiple relationships between ideas.

Boring - All Simple Sentences:

"I woke up. I got dressed. I ate breakfast. I went to school. The day was long. I came home. I did homework."

Problem: Choppy, repetitive, sounds like a shopping list!
Interesting - Varied Sentences:

"I woke up feeling tired. After getting dressed quickly, I grabbed some breakfast and rushed to school. The day dragged on, but eventually I came home and tackled my homework."

Why it's better: Mix of simple and complex sentences creates rhythm and flow.

Let's Practice: Sentence Length Variety

Mix short, medium, and long sentences for impact:

Short Sentences (1-5 words)

  • Create drama and tension
  • Emphasise important points
  • Speed up the pace

"The door creaked open. Silence. Then a scream."

Medium Sentences (6-15 words)

  • The most common - easy to read
  • Convey information clearly

Long Sentences (15+ words)

  • Build detail and description
  • Create flowing, elegant writing
  • Slow down the pace

"The forest stretched endlessly before us, its ancient trees towering overhead whilst dappled sunlight filtered through the leaves, creating dancing patterns on the moss-covered ground."

Going Further: Sentence Openers

Don't always start sentences with "The" or "I" or the subject. Try these:

-ing opener (verb):
  • "Running quickly, Tom caught the bus."
  • "Smiling nervously, she entered the room."
-ly opener (adverb):
  • "Carefully, she picked up the broken glass."
  • "Suddenly, the lights went out."
Preposition opener:
  • "After the match, we went for pizza."
  • "Beyond the gate, a mysterious garden awaited."
Subordinate clause opener:
  • "Although it was raining, the match continued."
  • "When the bell rang, everyone cheered."

Final Challenge: Your Writing Tasks

Task 1: Identify Sentence Types
Label each sentence as Simple, Compound, Complex, or Compound-Complex. Explain your choice.

a) The thunder rumbled ominously.
b) I love reading, but my brother prefers gaming.
c) When the concert ended, the crowd erupted in applause.
d) Although I was nervous, I gave my presentation, and everyone clapped.
e) Dogs make excellent pets.
Task 2: Transform Sentences
Take this simple sentence and rewrite it THREE times:

Simple sentence: "The girl ran home."

a) Rewrite as a compound sentence (add another main idea)
b) Rewrite as a complex sentence (add a subordinate clause)
c) Rewrite with an interesting sentence opener
Task 3: Improve Choppy Writing
Rewrite this paragraph combining and varying the sentences. Aim for at least THREE different sentence types.

Original: "The library was quiet. Students were reading. Some were on computers. The librarian walked past. She smiled at everyone. I was looking for a book. I couldn't find it. I asked for help."
Task 4: Use Different Openers
Write FOUR sentences about a stormy night. Each sentence must use a DIFFERENT opener type (-ing, -ly, preposition, subordinate clause). Label which type you've used.
Task 5: Create Pace with Length
Write a short action scene (60-80 words) where someone is being chased. Use SHORT sentences to create tension and speed, then ONE longer sentence when they escape to slow the pace down.
Task 6: Full Paragraph with Variety
Write a descriptive paragraph (100+ words) about your perfect day. Include:
• At least ONE simple sentence
• At least ONE compound sentence
• At least TWO complex sentences
• At least TWO different sentence openers
• A mix of short, medium, and long sentences
