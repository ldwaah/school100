Narrative Writing: Story Openings

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Story Beginnings

Hook Your Reader from the Start!

The opening of a story is crucial. It needs to grab the reader's attention and make them want to read on. In this resource, you'll discover different techniques to write engaging story openings that hook your audience from the very first line!

Getting Started: Why Story Openings Matter

The first few sentences of your story are the most important. They need to:

  • Grab the reader's attention immediately
  • Set the scene or introduce a character
  • Create curiosity or intrigue
  • Establish the tone (scary, funny, mysterious, exciting)
  • Make readers want to know what happens next
Boring Opening:

"My name is Tom. I am 12 years old. I live in London. This is a story about something that happened to me."

Why it's boring: Too much telling, no action, no intrigue, no atmosphere.
Exciting Opening:

"The footsteps behind me were getting closer. My heart pounded as I ran through the dark alley, knowing I had to reach home before midnight – or they would find me."

Why it works: Creates immediate tension, raises questions (Who's following? Why midnight?), uses action.

Let's Practice: Five Powerful Opening Techniques

1. Action Opening

Start in the middle of something happening.

Example:

"The car skidded off the road, tyres screeching, as Dad swerved to avoid the deer."

Effect: Immediately exciting and dramatic. Readers want to know what happens next.

2. Dialogue Opening

Start with someone speaking.

Example:

"You're not seriously going in there, are you?" whispered Maya, staring at the abandoned house.

Effect: Creates intrigue and introduces character voices naturally.

3. Setting Opening

Paint a vivid picture of where the story takes place.

Example:

"The forest was silent except for the crunch of dead leaves beneath my feet. Twisted branches reached across the path like skeletal fingers, blocking out the fading sunlight."

Effect: Creates atmosphere and sets the mood (in this case, spooky).

4. Mystery Opening

Start with something strange or unexplained.

Example:

"The box appeared on my doorstep with no address, no stamp, and no note – just my name written in silver ink."

Effect: Raises questions that make readers curious.

5. Character Opening

Introduce your main character in an interesting way.

Example:

"Nobody believed Jake when he said he could talk to animals, but the stray cat sitting on his windowsill knew better."

Effect: Tells us something intriguing about the character immediately.

Going Further: Opening Dos and Don'ts

DO:
  • Start with action, dialogue, or an interesting detail
  • Create questions in the reader's mind
  • Use vivid, descriptive language
  • Establish your story's tone quickly
  • Make every word count
DON'T:
  • Start with "My name is..." or "I'm going to tell you about..."
  • Begin with someone waking up (unless it's really unusual)
  • Give too much background information straight away
  • Use boring, predictable phrases
  • Explain everything – leave some mystery!
