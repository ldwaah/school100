Information Writing: Reports & Articles

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Non-Fiction Writing

Share Information Clearly!

Information writing teaches readers about a topic in a clear, organised way. Whether you're writing a school report, a newspaper article, or explaining how something works, you need to be accurate, structured, and interesting. Let's master this essential skill!

Getting Started: Features of Information Writing

1. Clear Structure

  • Introduction: What are you writing about?
  • Main paragraphs: Different aspects of the topic
  • Conclusion: Summary or final thoughts
  • Use headings and subheadings

2. Factual Language

  • Present facts, not opinions
  • Use statistics and data
  • Avoid "I think" or "in my opinion"
  • Be objective and balanced

3. Present Tense

  • Use present tense for facts: "Lions live in Africa"
  • Past tense for historical information: "World War II ended in 1945"

4. Formal Tone

  • Third person (he, she, it, they)
  • No contractions (write "do not" instead of "don't")
  • Professional vocabulary

5. Technical Vocabulary

  • Use subject-specific terms
  • Define difficult words if necessary
  • Show you understand the topic

Let's Practice: Report vs Article

Information Report

Purpose: To classify and describe information systematically

Example Topics:

The Water Cycle, Roman Society, Endangered Species, The Human Heart

Features:

Very formal, objective, clear categories, bullet points or numbered lists common, diagrams helpful

Information Article

Purpose: To inform but in a more engaging way (e.g., for newspapers or magazines)

Example Topics:

How Social Media Affects Teenagers, The Rise of Electric Cars, Climate Change Explained

Features:

Slightly less formal, catchy headline, engaging opening, quotes from experts, still factual

Going Further: Writing an Engaging Opening

Techniques for Openings:
  • Interesting fact: "Did you know that the human brain has 86 billion neurons?"
  • Surprising statistic: "Over 8 million tonnes of plastic enter our oceans every year."
  • Question: "What causes earthquakes and why are some regions more at risk?"
  • Definition: "Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert sunlight into energy."

Final Challenge: Organising Information

Group related information into clear paragraphs with topic sentences:

Example - Report on Dolphins:

Paragraph 1: Introduction - What dolphins are (marine mammals)

Paragraph 2: Appearance - Physical description

Paragraph 3: Habitat - Where they live

Paragraph 4: Diet - What they eat

Paragraph 5: Behaviour - How they communicate and interact

Paragraph 6: Conclusion - Summary or conservation status

Your Writing Tasks

Task 1: Identify Features
Read this information paragraph and list FIVE features of information writing that you can spot (e.g., present tense, facts, formal language, third person, technical vocabulary).

"Earthquakes occur when tectonic plates beneath the Earth's surface shift suddenly. This movement releases energy in the form of seismic waves, which travel through the Earth and cause the ground to shake. The point beneath the surface where the earthquake originates is called the hypocentre, whilst the point directly above it on the surface is the epicentre. Scientists measure earthquake magnitude using the Richter scale."
Task 2: Write an Engaging Opening
Choose ONE topic and write an engaging opening paragraph (40-60 words) for an information article. Use one of the techniques from Part 3.

Topics: The Solar System, The History of Football, How Mobile Phones Work, Life in Ancient Egypt
Task 3: Plan a Report
Choose an animal (e.g., tiger, penguin, elephant, shark) and create a plan for an information report about it. Write 6 paragraph headings showing how you'd organise the information. Include a brief note about what each paragraph would cover.
Task 4: Write an Information Paragraph
Write ONE detailed paragraph (70-90 words) explaining how the water cycle works. Use formal language, present tense, facts, and technical vocabulary (evaporation, condensation, precipitation).
Task 5: News Article
Write a short information article (100-120 words) about a recent school event (sports day, charity fundraiser, school trip, or make one up). Include:
• A catchy headline
• An engaging opening
• Facts and details
• A quote from someone involved (teacher or student)
Task 6: Full Information Report
Write a complete information report (150-180 words) on ONE of these topics:
• The importance of recycling
• How volcanoes form
• The Vikings
• Endangered species

Include: introduction, at least 3 main paragraphs with clear topics, conclusion. Use appropriate features (headings, facts, formal tone).
