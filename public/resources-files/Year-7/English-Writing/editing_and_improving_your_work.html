Editing & Improving Your Work

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Proofreading & Polishing

Good Writers Always Edit!

First drafts are never perfect - that's completely normal! Professional writers spend lots of time editing and improving their work. This resource focuses on how to check, correct, and upgrade your writing to make it the best it can be!

Exploring Editing Process - COPS

Use the COPS method to check your work systematically:

C - CAPITALS

  • Start of sentences
  • Proper nouns (names of people, places)
  • The pronoun "I"
  • Days, months, holidays

O - ORGANISATION

  • Clear paragraphs (TiPToP)
  • Logical structure
  • Good flow between ideas
  • Appropriate opening and ending

P - PUNCTUATION

  • Full stops at sentence ends
  • Commas in lists and complex sentences
  • Question marks and exclamation marks
  • Speech marks for dialogue
  • Apostrophes for possession and contractions

S - SPELLING

  • Common words spelled correctly
  • Homophones used correctly (their/there/they're)
  • Subject-specific vocabulary
  • Check words you're unsure about

Let's Practice: Upgrading Your Vocabulary

Basic Vocabulary:

"The boy went to the shop. He got some nice things. It was good."

Problems: Boring words (went, got, nice, good, things)
Upgraded Vocabulary:

"The boy strolled to the corner shop. He purchased several delicious treats. It was a pleasant experience."

Why it's better: More interesting, specific verbs and adjectives.
Words to Avoid & Better Alternatives:
  • nice → pleasant, delightful, enjoyable, lovely, charming
  • good → excellent, superb, fantastic, wonderful, impressive
  • bad → terrible, awful, dreadful, disappointing, poor
  • got → obtained, received, acquired, collected, purchased
  • went → travelled, journeyed, strolled, wandered, headed
  • said → whispered, shouted, exclaimed, muttered, announced

Going Further: Adding Detail

Make sentences more interesting by adding:

Adjectives (describing words)

Basic: "The dog barked."

With adjectives: "The fierce, black dog barked."

Adverbs (how something is done)

Basic: "She ran home."

With adverb: "She ran home quickly."

Expanded noun phrases

Basic: "The house was old."

Expanded: "The crumbling Victorian house, with its peeling paint and broken windows, stood alone on the hill."

Final Challenge: Common Mistakes to Fix

1. Sentence Fragments

Wrong: "Because I was tired."

Fixed: "I went to bed early because I was tired."

2. Run-on Sentences

Wrong: "I woke up I ate breakfast I went to school."

Fixed: "I woke up, ate breakfast, and went to school."

3. Repetition

Wrong: "The cat was a black cat. The cat was very big. I liked the cat."

Fixed: "The cat was large and black. I really liked it."

4. Vague Pronouns

Unclear: "Tom and Jake went shopping. He bought trainers."

Who bought them - Tom or Jake?

Clear: "Tom and Jake went shopping. Tom bought trainers."

Task 3: Add Detail
Take these basic sentences and expand them by adding adjectives, adverbs, and extra detail. Make each at least twice as long.

a) The man walked down the street.
b) The storm began.
c) My room is messy.
Task 4: Fix Sentence Problems
Identify the problem (fragment, run-on, repetition, or vague pronoun) and rewrite each correctly:

a) Although the weather was terrible.
b) My sister loves reading she reads every night she has hundreds of books.
c) The book was on the table. The book was interesting. I read the book.
d) Sarah and Emma went shopping. She bought a dress.
Task 5: Write and Edit
Write a paragraph (80-100 words) about your last holiday or weekend. Then, use COPS to check it. List what you checked and any changes you made.
