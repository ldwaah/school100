Persuasive Writing: Making Your Point

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Argument & Opinion

Win People Over with Words!

Persuasive writing is about convincing someone to agree with your opinion or take action. Whether you're writing a letter, a speech, or an argument, you need strong techniques to make your case. Today, you'll master the art of persuasion!

Exploring Structure of Persuasive Writing

A strong persuasive text follows this structure:

1. Introduction:
  • State your opinion clearly
  • Grab the reader's attention with a question, fact, or bold statement
2. Main Arguments (3-4 paragraphs):
  • Each paragraph = one reason supporting your opinion
  • Include evidence, examples, or facts
  • Use persuasive techniques
3. Counter-argument (optional but powerful):
  • Acknowledge the opposite view
  • Explain why your view is still better
4. Conclusion:
  • Restate your opinion
  • Summarise your strongest points
  • End with a call to action (what you want readers to do)

Let's Practice: Persuasive Techniques - AFORREST

Use these powerful techniques to strengthen your argument:

A - Alliteration

Repeating the same sound at the start of words for emphasis.

Example: "School should start later to stop sleepy, stressed students."

F - Facts

Use statistics or proven information to support your argument.

Example: "Studies show that 90% of teenagers don't get enough sleep."

O - Opinions (presented as expert views)

Quote experts or authority figures.

Example: "Doctors agree that teenagers need 9 hours of sleep per night."

R - Rhetorical Questions

Questions you don't expect an answer to - they make readers think.

Example: "How can we expect students to learn when they're exhausted?"

R - Repetition

Repeat key words or phrases for emphasis.

Example: "We need change. We deserve change. We demand change."

E - Emotive Language

Words that create strong emotions.

Example: "Students are suffering, struggling, and sacrificing their wellbeing."

S - Statistics

Numbers and data add credibility.

Example: "75% of students report feeling tired during lessons."

T - The Rule of Three

List things in threes - it's memorable and powerful.

Example: "Starting school later would improve health, grades, and happiness."

Going Further: More Persuasive Techniques

Direct Address

Use "you" to speak directly to the reader.

"You know how frustrating it is when..."

"Imagine if you could..."

Personal Anecdotes

Share relatable stories or examples.

"Last term, I witnessed a student fall asleep in class due to exhaustion..."

Modal Verbs (certainty words)

Use words like must, should, will, need to, have to.

"We must act now."

"Schools should reconsider their policies."

Final Challenge: Your Writing Tasks

Task 1: Identify Techniques
Read this persuasive paragraph and identify which AFORREST techniques are used. List them and provide the example from the text.

Paragraph: "How can we let plastic pollution poison our planet? Every year, 8 million tonnes of plastic enter our oceans, killing countless creatures. Marine biologists warn that by 2050, there will be more plastic than fish in the sea. We must act now. We need to reduce, reuse, and recycle. Don't you want a cleaner, safer, healthier world for future generations?"
Task 2: Write an Introduction
Choose ONE topic and write a persuasive introduction (3-4 sentences). State your opinion clearly and use at least TWO AFORREST techniques.

Topics:
a) School uniform should/shouldn't be compulsory
b) Homework should/shouldn't be given at weekends
c) Teenagers should/shouldn't have mobile phones in school
Task 3: Write Three Argument Paragraphs
Using the same topic from Task 2, write THREE separate paragraphs, each giving a different reason to support your opinion. Each paragraph should be 40-60 words and use persuasive techniques.
Task 4: Counter-Argument
Now write a counter-argument paragraph. Acknowledge what people who disagree might say, then explain why your view is still correct. (40-60 words)
Task 5: Powerful Conclusion
Write a conclusion for your persuasive piece. Restate your opinion, summarise key points, and include a strong call to action. Use emotive language and repetition. (40-60 words)
Task 6: Full Persuasive Letter
Write a persuasive letter (at least 150 words) to your local council arguing for ONE improvement to your area. Structure it properly with introduction, arguments, and conclusion. Use at least FIVE different AFORREST techniques.

Ideas: Better public transport, more youth clubs, safer cycling lanes, improved parks, later library hours
