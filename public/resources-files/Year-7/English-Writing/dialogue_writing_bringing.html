Dialogue Writing: Bringing Characters to Life

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Speech & Conversation

Make Your Characters Talk!

Dialogue brings stories to life. When written well, it reveals personality, moves the plot forward, and makes characters feel real. Today, you'll master the rules of dialogue punctuation and learn how to write natural, engaging conversations!

Getting Started: Dialogue Punctuation Rules

Getting dialogue punctuation right is essential. Follow these rules:

Rule 1: Speech marks go around the spoken words

"I can't believe we won!" shouted Amira.

Rule 2: Punctuation goes INSIDE the speech marks

"Where are you going?" asked Tom.

"I don't know," she replied.

Rule 3: Start a new line for each new speaker

"Did you finish your homework?" Mum asked.
"Not yet," I admitted.

Rule 4: Use commas with speech tags (said, asked, replied)

"I'm starving," said Jake.
Sarah whispered, "Me too."

Rule 5: Use exclamation or question marks when appropriate

"Watch out!" yelled Emma.
"What's happening?" asked Ben.

Incorrect:

Tom said I'm going home.
Sarah replied, Where are you going.

Problems: No speech marks, wrong punctuation.
Correct:

"I'm going home," said Tom.
"Where are you going?" replied Sarah.

Let's Practice: Alternatives to 'Said'

Using varied dialogue tags makes writing more interesting:

When someone speaks quietly:
  • whispered, murmured, muttered, mumbled
When someone speaks loudly:
  • shouted, yelled, bellowed, announced, exclaimed
When someone asks:
  • asked, questioned, enquired, wondered
When someone answers:
  • replied, answered, responded, retorted
When someone is emotional:
  • cried, sobbed, laughed, giggled, sighed, groaned, snapped

Going Further: Writing Natural Dialogue

Good dialogue sounds like real people talking. Here's how:

Use contractions and informal language

Unnatural:

"I cannot believe that we are going to be late. We will have to hurry."

Natural:

"I can't believe we're gonna be late. We'll have to hurry!"

Keep it short and snappy

Too long:

"Well, I think that we should probably consider going to the cinema because there is a new film out that everyone has been talking about and it looks really good and I heard the reviews are excellent," said Emma.

Better:

"Want to see that new film?" asked Emma. "Everyone's raving about it."

Include interruptions and reactions

Realistic conversation:

"I was thinking we could—"
"Wait, did you hear that?" Jake interrupted.
"Hear what?"
"That noise. From upstairs."

Show personality through speech

  • A nervous person might use "um", "maybe", "I think"
  • A confident person uses direct statements
  • A bossy person gives commands
  • Different ages speak differently (teenagers vs adults)

Final Challenge: Your Writing Tasks

Task 1: Fix the Punctuation
Rewrite these sentences with correct dialogue punctuation:

a) Are you coming to the party Tom asked
b) I dont know Sarah replied maybe
c) You have to come everyone will be there he said
d) Okay I'll think about it she sighed
Task 2: Replace 'Said'
Rewrite this conversation replacing every 'said' with a more interesting dialogue tag. Make sure it fits the tone.

"Help!" said Emma.
"What's wrong?" said Jake.
"There's a huge spider in my room," said Emma.
"Where?" said Jake.
"On the ceiling. Get it away!" said Emma.
Task 3: Write a Two-Person Conversation
Write a conversation (at least 8 lines of dialogue) between two friends who disagree about something. Show their personalities through how they speak. Use correct punctuation and varied dialogue tags.

Topic ideas: Where to go at the weekend, which film to watch, whether homework is important, best type of pet
Task 4: Add Dialogue to Narrative
Read this paragraph with NO dialogue. Rewrite it including conversation to make it more interesting (at least 100 words).

Paragraph: "Mia and her brother found an old map in the attic. They decided to follow it. They thought it might lead to treasure. They were excited but also a bit scared. They wondered if they should tell their parents."
Task 5: Three-Way Conversation
Write a conversation between THREE people (at least 10 lines of dialogue total). The scenario: three students working on a group project, but one person hasn't done their part. Make each person's personality clear through their dialogue.
Task 6: Dialogue Analysis
Think of a conversation from a book, film, or TV show that you remember. What made it interesting or memorable? Was it funny, dramatic, mysterious? Describe the conversation and explain what made it effective.
