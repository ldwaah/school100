Paragraphing & Structure Skills

Year 7 English - Writing 60 minutes Organisation Skills

Organise Your Writing Like a Pro!

Good paragraphing makes your writing clear and easy to follow. This lesson will help you understand when to start a new paragraph, how to structure paragraphs properly, and how to link ideas smoothly so your writing flows beautifully!

Understanding a Paragraph

A paragraph is a group of sentences about ONE main idea. When you move to a new idea, you start a new paragraph.

Poor Paragraphing (No breaks):

I woke up feeling excited because it was my birthday. I ran downstairs and saw presents on the table. My mum had made pancakes for breakfast. After eating, I opened my presents. I got a new bike which was blue and shiny. Then my friends arrived for my party. We played games in the garden. Everyone had fun.

Problem: Too many different ideas in one paragraph. Hard to read!
Good Paragraphing (Clear breaks):

Paragraph 1: I woke up feeling excited because it was my birthday. I ran downstairs and saw presents on the table. My mum had made pancakes for breakfast.

Paragraph 2: After eating, I opened my presents. I got a new bike which was blue and shiny. It was exactly what I'd wanted.

Paragraph 3: Then my friends arrived for my party. We played games in the garden and everyone had fun.

Why it's better: Each paragraph focuses on ONE part of the day (morning, presents, party).

Let's Practice: When to Start a New Paragraph - TiPToP

Remember the acronym TiPToP to know when to start a new paragraph:

Ti - TIME changes

When the time changes (later that day, the next morning, after lunch)

P - PERSON changes

When a different person starts speaking or when focus shifts to a new character

T - TOPIC changes

When you move to a new idea or subject

oP - PLACE changes

When the setting or location changes

Example showing TiPToP in action:

Paragraph 1 (Place 1 - School): The bell rang for the end of lessons. Students poured out of classrooms, chattering excitedly about the weekend ahead.

Paragraph 2 (Place 2 - Home): At home, Mia dumped her school bag by the door and headed straight to the kitchen. The smell of baking filled the house.

Paragraph 3 (Person changes - focus on Mum): Her mum looked up from the oven with a smile. "Perfect timing," she said, pulling out a tray of cookies.

Going Further: Paragraph Structure - PEEL

Each paragraph should have a clear structure. Use PEEL:

P - POINT

Start with your main idea (topic sentence)

E - EVIDENCE/EXAMPLE

Give and example or detail to support your point

E - EXPLAIN

Explain or develop your idea further

L - LINK

Link to your next paragraph or back to the main topic

PEEL in action:

POINT: The forest was and eerie place at night.

EVIDENCE: Shadows danced between the trees and strange rustling sounds echoed from every direction.

EXPLAIN: Every crack of a twig made my heart race, and I couldn't shake the feeling that something was watching me.

LINK: That's why I decided to turn back before it got any darker.

Final Challenge: Linking Words and Phrases

Use connectives to link ideas smoothly within and between paragraphs:

Time connectives:
  • Firstly, Secondly, Finally
  • Later, Meanwhile, Afterwards, Eventually
  • The next day, After a while, Soon
Adding information:
  • Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally
  • Also, In addition, As well as this
Contrasting ideas:
  • However, On the other hand, Nevertheless
  • Although, Despite this, In contrast
Cause and effect:
  • Therefore, Consequently, As a result
  • Because of this, For this reason

Your Writing Tasks

Task 1: Split into Paragraphs
Read this text with no paragraph breaks. Rewrite it with proper paragraphing. Explain why you started each new paragraph (using TiPToP).

Text: "The library was completely silent. Everyone was studying for their exams. Sarah looked at the clock nervously. She only had two days left to revise everything. 'How's it going?' whispered Tom from across the table. Sarah shrugged and returned to her textbook. Outside, rain began to fall heavily. The sound was oddly comforting. It made the library feel even cosier. The librarian walked past their table and smiled. Sarah felt a little better. She could do this."
Task 2: Write a PEEL Paragraph
Choose ONE of these topic sentences and write a full PEEL paragraph (4-5 sentences).

Choose one:
a) Social media can be both helpful and harmful to young people.
b) Regular exercise is important for everyone.
c) Reading books develops imagination and creativity.
Task 3: Add Linking Words
Rewrite these sentences adding appropriate linking words to improve the flow:

a) I wanted to go to the party. I had too much homework. I stayed home.

b) We arrived at the beach. We set up our towels. We went swimming. We had lunch.

c) The experiment failed the first time. We tried again. We changed the temperature. We succeeded.
Task 4: Narrative with Proper Paragraphs
Write a short story (at least 150 words) about finding something mysterious. Use AT LEAST 4 paragraphs with clear TiPToP breaks. Show where each paragraph starts.
Task 5: Persuasive Writing with Structure
Write 3 PEEL paragraphs (about 120 words total) persuading your head teacher to make a change at school (longer lunch, later start time, no homework Fridays, etc.). Each paragraph should give a different reason.
Task 6: Identify Paragraph Problems
Read this paragraph and identify what's wrong with it. How would you fix it?

Paragraph: "Dogs are great pets. My favourite food is pizza. Last summer I went to Spain. Dogs need regular exercise and lots of attention. The weather was really hot. I think everyone should have a dog. I also like pasta."
