Understanding Fractions!

Fractions show parts of a whole. They're everywhere in real life - from sharing pizza to measuring ingredients. In this resource, you'll discover what fractions mean, how to read and write them, and how to compare them!

Understanding a Fraction

A fraction shows how many parts of a whole you have.

Parts of a Fraction

Numerator (top number) = how many parts you have

Denominator (bottom number) = how many equal parts the whole is divided into

Example: 3/4

• Numerator = 3 (you have 3 parts)

• Denominator = 4 (the whole is divided into 4 equal parts)

• This means: 3 out of 4 equal parts

Real Life Examples:

• A pizza cut into 8 slices. You eat 3 slices = 3/8 of the pizza

• A chocolate bar with 12 squares. You eat 5 squares = 5/12 of the bar

• An hour has 60 minutes. 15 minutes = 15/60 = 1/4 of an hour

Let's Practice: Types of Fractions

Proper Fractions

Numerator is smaller than denominator (less than 1 whole)

Examples: 1/2, 3/4, 2/5, 7/8

Improper Fractions

Numerator is equal to or larger than denominator (1 whole or more)

Examples: 5/4, 7/3, 8/8, 10/5

Mixed Numbers

A whole number and a proper fraction together

Examples: 1 1/2, 2 3/4, 3 1/5

1 1/2 means: 1 whole + 1/2

Going Further: Equivalent Fractions

Different fractions that represent the same amount.

Example:

1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8

All these fractions represent the same amount (half of something)

How to find equivalent fractions: Multiply or divide both numerator and denominator by the same number

1/2 × 2/2 = 2/4

1/2 × 4/4 = 4/8

Key Rule:
  • Whatever you do to the numerator, do the same to the denominator
  • 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8 = 5/10
  • To simplify: find a number that divides into both numerator and denominator

Final Challenge: Comparing Fractions

Same Denominator

Compare numerators - bigger numerator = bigger fraction

Example: 3/8 vs 5/8

5/8 is bigger because 5 > 3

Same Numerator

Compare denominators - smaller denominator = bigger fraction

Example: 3/4 vs 3/8

3/4 is bigger because when you divide into fewer parts, each part is bigger

Different Numerators and Denominators

Find equivalent fractions with the same denominator, then compare

Example: Compare 1/2 and 3/8

1/2 = 4/8, so 1/2 (4/8) > 3/8

Task 6: Word Problems
Solve these word problems. Show your working:

a) A pizza is cut into 8 slices. Tom eats 3 slices. What fraction of the pizza did he eat?
b) Sarah has a chocolate bar with 12 squares. She gives 5 squares to her friend. What fraction does she have left?
c) A class has 24 students. 9 students are boys. What fraction of the class are girls?
d) A cake is divided into 6 equal pieces. If 2 pieces are eaten, what fraction remains?
