Decimals: Understanding Decimal Numbers

Master Decimal Numbers!

Decimals are numbers with a decimal point. They're used for money, measurements, and many real-life situations. This resource focuses on to read, write, compare, and calculate with decimals!

Understanding Decimals

Decimals show parts smaller than 1 whole. The decimal point separates whole numbers from parts.

Place Value in Decimals

Example: 23.456

• 2 = tens (20)

• 3 = ones (3)

• . = decimal point

• 4 = tenths (0.4 or 4/10)

• 5 = hundredths (0.05 or 5/100)

• 6 = thousandths (0.006 or 6/1000)

Reading Decimals:

• 0.5 = "zero point five" or "five tenths"

• 2.34 = "two point three four" or "two and thirty-four hundredths"

• 10.07 = "ten point zero seven" or "ten and seven hundredths"

Let's Practice: Decimals and Fractions

Decimals and fractions show the same thing in different ways.

Converting Fractions to Decimals

Divide the numerator by the denominator

Examples:

• 1/2 = 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5

• 3/4 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75

• 1/10 = 1 ÷ 10 = 0.1

• 7/100 = 7 ÷ 100 = 0.07

Converting Decimals to Fractions

Write the decimal as a fraction with the right denominator

Examples:

• 0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2

• 0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4

• 0.75 = 75/100 = 3/4

• 0.1 = 1/10

Going Further: Adding and Subtracting Decimals

Column Method (line up decimal points):

Example: 3.45 + 2.7

  3.45
+ 2.70
────
  6.15

Important: Always line up the decimal points!

Example: 5.8 - 2.34

  5.80
- 2.34
────
  3.46

Top Tips:
  • Line up decimal points vertically
  • Add zeros if needed to make place values match
  • Work from right to left, just like whole numbers
  • Don't forget the decimal point in your answer!

Final Challenge: Comparing Decimals

Step-by-Step Comparison

Example: Compare 2.34 and 2.7

Step 1: Compare whole number parts: 2 = 2 (same)

Step 2: Compare tenths: 3 < 7

Answer: 2.34 < 2.7

Making Decimals the Same Length

Add zeros to make comparing easier

2.34 = 2.340

2.7 = 2.700

Now compare: 340 < 700, so 2.34 < 2.7
