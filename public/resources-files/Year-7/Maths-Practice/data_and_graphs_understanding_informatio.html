Read and Understand Data!

Data and graphs help us understand information. Throughout this activity, you'll develop to read tables, understand bar charts, and work with simple statistics like mean, mode, and median!

Getting Started: Reading Tables

A table organizes information in rows and columns.

Understanding Tables

Example Table:

Favourite Sport | Number of Students
Football | 12
Basketball | 8
Tennis | 5
Swimming | 10

This table shows how many students like each sport.

How to Read Tables:

• Look at the column headings to understand what the data shows

• Read across rows to see complete information

• Add up numbers to find totals

Let's Practice: Mean, Mode, and Median

These are ways to describe a set of numbers.

Mean (Average)

Add all numbers together, then divide by how many numbers there are

Example: Scores: 5, 7, 8, 6, 9

Mean = (5 + 7 + 8 + 6 + 9) ÷ 5 = 35 ÷ 5 = 7

Mode

The number that appears most often

Example: Scores: 5, 7, 7, 6, 7

Mode = 7 (appears 3 times)

Median

The middle number when numbers are in order

Example: Scores: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9

Median = 7 (the middle number)

Remember:
  • Mean = average (add up, divide by count)
  • Mode = most common number
  • Median = middle number (put in order first)
  • Always show your working!

Going Further: Your Practice Tasks

Task 1: Reading Tables
Use this table to answer the questions:

Favourite Colour | Number of Students
Red | 8
Blue | 12
Green | 5
Yellow | 6

a) How many students chose blue?
b) How many students chose red or green?
c) What is the total number of students?
d) Which colour was chosen by the most students?
Task 2: Finding the Mean
Find the mean (average) of each set of numbers. Show your working:

a) 5, 7, 9, 11, 8
b) 12, 15, 18, 15
c) 20, 25, 30, 25, 20
d) 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 10
Task 3: Finding the Mode
Find the mode (most common number) of each set. Show your working:

a) 5, 7, 5, 9, 5
b) 12, 15, 12, 18, 15, 12
c) 8, 10, 8, 12, 10, 8
d) 3, 5, 3, 7, 5, 3, 9
Task 4: Finding the Median
Find the median (middle number) of each set. Put numbers in order first, then find the middle. Show your working:

a) 5, 9, 7, 3, 11
b) 15, 8, 12, 20, 10
c) 25, 30, 20, 35, 15
d) 6, 12, 8, 10, 4, 14
Task 5: Word Problems
Solve these word problems. Show your working:

a) Tom scored 8, 9, 7, 10, and 6 in his maths tests. What is his mean score?
b) The ages of students in a group are: 12, 13, 12, 14, 12, 13. What is the mode age?
c) The heights (in cm) of 5 students are: 150, 145, 160, 155, 150. What is the median height?
d) A class collected these amounts of money: £5, £8, £6, £10, £7. What is the mean amount?

Reflection

Reflection:
What is the difference between mean, mode, and median? How do you find the mean of a set of numbers? When would you use a table to show data? Can you think of three real-life situations where you would use data and statistics?
