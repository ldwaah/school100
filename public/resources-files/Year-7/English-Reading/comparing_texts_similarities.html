Comparing Texts: Similarities and Differences

Year 7 English - Reading 60 minutes Comparison Skills

Spot the Connections!

Comparing texts means looking at how they are similar and different. This skill helps you understand different perspectives, analyse writing styles, and think critically. Today, you'll become an expert at comparing texts!

Getting Started: What to Compare

When comparing texts, look at several key areas:

Areas to Compare:
  • Topic/Subject: What are they about? Same or different?
  • Purpose: Why were they written? (inform, persuade, entertain)
  • Audience: Who are they for? (children, adults, experts)
  • Tone: How do they sound? (serious, funny, friendly, formal)
  • Language: What words and sentences are used?
  • Structure: How are they organised?
  • Viewpoint: What opinion or perspective is presented?
Useful Comparing Words:
  • Similarities: both, similarly, likewise, also, too, in the same way
  • Differences: however, whereas, unlike, in contrast, on the other hand, but

Let's Practice: First Comparison - Pet Descriptions

Read these two descriptions and compare them.

Text A: Charlie the Cat

Charlie is a fluffy ginger cat with bright green eyes and a white patch on his chest. He loves sleeping in sunny spots and purring loudly when you stroke him. Every morning, he waits by the kitchen door for his breakfast, meowing impatiently. Charlie is gentle with children and enjoys playing with feather toys. At night, he curls up at the foot of the bed, keeping everyone's feet warm.

Text B: Max the Dog

Max is an energetic black Labrador with a wagging tail that never stops. He bounds around the park, chasing tennis balls with endless enthusiasm. Every morning, he barks excitedly at the door, desperate for his walk. Max loves meeting new people and dogs, jumping up to greet everyone. At night, he sprawls across the living room floor, exhausted from his adventures.

Question 1:
What is similar about both texts? Find at least TWO similarities.
Question 2:
What is different about the two pets? Describe TWO differences.
Question 3:
How is the tone of both texts similar? What mood do both writers create?

Question 4:
Both texts are advertisements. What is the purpose of both? What do they both want readers to do?
Question 5:
How are the two holidays different? Compare what each one offers.
Question 6:
Compare the language used in both adverts. How do both writers try to persuade readers? Give examples.

Final Challenge: News Articles

Read these two articles about the same event and compare them.

Text A: Local News Report

Student Raises £2,000 for Charity

Year 9 student Emma Johnson completed a sponsored 10-kilometre run on Saturday, raising an impressive £2,000 for Cancer Research UK. The event took place at Riverside Park and attracted over 100 participants.

Emma, 14, trained for three months to prepare for the challenge. "I wanted to help because my grandmother is currently receiving cancer treatment," she explained. "Every pound raised could help save lives."

The fundraiser was organised by Westfield Academy, with support from local businesses who donated prizes for a raffle. Emma's headteacher, Mrs. Roberts, praised her dedication and fundraising success.

Text B: School Newsletter

Amazing Emma - Our Fundraising Hero!

We are incredibly proud to announce that our fantastic Year 9 student, Emma Johnson, has raised a phenomenal £2,000 for Cancer Research UK!

Emma's inspirational achievement came after months of determined training. Supported by her family, friends, and teachers, she completed a challenging 10km run last Saturday. Over 100 people joined the event, creating a wonderful community atmosphere.

Emma's motivation came from a very personal place – supporting her beloved grandmother through cancer treatment. Her dedication shows what can be achieved when students put their minds to something meaningful.

Well done, Emma! You're an absolute star and a brilliant role model for our whole school community!

Question 7:
What information appears in BOTH texts? List THREE facts that are the same in both.
Question 8:
How is the tone different in the two texts? Compare how each writer presents Emma's achievement.
Question 9:
Compare the language used in both texts. Find examples of words that show the different tones.
Question 10:
Both texts are about the same event but written for different audiences. Who is each text written for? How can you tell?
