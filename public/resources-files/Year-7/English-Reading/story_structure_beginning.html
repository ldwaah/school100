Story Structure: Beginning, Middle, End

Year 7 English - Reading 60 minutes Plot Structure

How Stories Work

Every good story has a structure – a beginning, middle, and end. Understanding how stories are built helps you appreciate them more and write better stories yourself. In this resource, you'll discover to spot the three main parts of any story!

Exploring Three Parts of a Story

Stories are like journeys. They have a starting point, adventures along the way, and a destination.

Beginning (Introduction)

This is where we meet the characters and learn about their world.

  • Who are the main characters?
  • Where and when does the story happen?
  • What is normal life like for them?
  • What problem or challenge appears?

Middle (Development)

This is the longest part where the main action happens.

  • What does the character do about the problem?
  • What obstacles or challenges do they face?
  • Does the problem get worse before it gets better?
  • How do the characters change or grow?

End (Resolution)

This is where the story wraps up and problems are solved.

  • How is the problem solved?
  • What happens to the characters?
  • Has anything changed from the beginning?
  • Is there a lesson or message?
Top Tips for Understanding Story Structure:
  • The beginning is usually short – just enough to set the scene
  • The middle is the longest part with the most action
  • The ending brings everything to a close
  • Look for the main problem and how it's solved
Example: "The Lost Puppy"

Beginning: Emma finds a lost puppy in the park. It has no collar.

Middle: Emma tries to find the owner. She makes posters, asks neighbours, and checks with vets. No one claims the puppy. Emma grows attached to it.

End: Emma's parents agree she can keep the puppy. She names him Lucky. Emma learns about responsibility and caring for pets.
