Making Predictions: What Happens Next?

Year 7 English - Reading 60 minutes Prediction Skills

Become a Story Detective!

Good readers don't just read words – they think ahead! When you make predictions, you use clues from the story to guess what might happen next. This keeps reading exciting and helps you understand stories better. This resource focuses on to predict like a pro!

Getting Started: How to Make Good Predictions

Making predictions means using clues to guess what might happen next in a story. Good predictions aren't just wild guesses – they're based on evidence!

Clues to Look For:
  • Character's behaviour: How are they acting? What do their actions suggest?
  • Story patterns: Have you read similar stories before?
  • Hints from the writer: Are there clues about future events?
  • Problems building up: Is something going wrong that needs fixing?
  • Your own knowledge: What would happen in real life?
Example Prediction:

Story Extract: "Lucy checked her bag for the third time. The concert tickets were still there. She'd been looking forward to this for months. But as she reached the station, dark clouds rolled in. Thunder rumbled in the distance."

Prediction: It will probably rain, which might cause problems getting to the concert. Maybe the train will be delayed or cancelled.

Evidence: Dark clouds, thunder, and the fact that she's been looking forward to it suggests something will go wrong to create drama in the story.

Top Tips for Predicting:
  • Use what you already know from the story
  • Think about what would make sense
  • Look for hints and clues
  • It's okay if your prediction is wrong – good readers adjust their thinking!
  • Explain why you think something will happen
