Fact vs Opinion: Spotting the Difference

Year 7 English - Reading 60 minutes Critical Reading

Truth or Belief?

Every day, we read things online, in newspapers, and in books. Some statements are facts that can be proven true. Others are opinions – personal beliefs or feelings. Learning to tell the difference helps you become a smarter, more critical reader!

Getting Started: What's the Difference?

FACT: A statement that can be proven true or false. You can check it with evidence, measurements, or records.

Examples: "Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius." "London is the capital of England." "There are 7 days in a week."

OPINION: A statement that expresses someone's personal belief, feeling, or judgement. Different people can have different opinions.

Examples: "Chocolate ice cream is the best flavour." "Winter is better than summer." "Football is more exciting than cricket."

How to Spot Facts:
  • Can be checked with evidence or research
  • Include numbers, dates, or measurements
  • State things that actually happened
  • Everyone would agree if they checked
How to Spot Opinions:
  • Include feeling words (love, hate, enjoy, boring, amazing)
  • Use judgement words (best, worst, better, should, must)
  • Can't be proven right or wrong
  • Different people might disagree
  • Often start with "I think..." or "I believe..."
Practice Examples:

Statement: "Dogs make better pets than cats."
Fact or Opinion? OPINION – This is a personal belief. Some people prefer cats!

Statement: "Dogs are mammals."
Fact or Opinion? FACT – This can be proven with science.

Statement: "The school day starts at 8:45am."
Fact or Opinion? FACT – This can be checked on the timetable.

Statement: "School uniforms are uncomfortable."
Fact or Opinion? OPINION – Some students might find them comfortable.
