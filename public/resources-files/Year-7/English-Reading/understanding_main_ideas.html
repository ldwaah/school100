Understanding Main Ideas: Key Messages

Year 7 English - Reading 60 minutes Comprehension

Finding the Heart of the Story

Every piece of writing has a main idea – the most important message or point the writer wants to share. Learning to spot main ideas helps you understand what you read quickly and remember it better. Today, you'll become and expert at finding the key messages in any text!

Getting Started: Main Ideas vs Details

Understanding the difference between main ideas and supporting details is crucial for good reading comprehension.

MAIN IDEA:

The most important point or message in a text. It's what the whole passage is mainly about. Usually it can be summed up in one sentence.

SUPPORTING DETAILS:

Extra information that explains, describes, or gives examples about the main idea. These are the smaller facts and descriptions.

Example Paragraph:

"Recycling is important for protecting our planet. When we recycle paper, we save trees from being cut down. Recycling plastic reduces pollution in our oceans. Glass and metal can be melted down and used again instead of taking up space in landfills. Every item we recycle makes a difference."

Main Idea: Recycling is important for protecting our planet.

Supporting Details:

  • Recycling paper saves trees
  • Recycling plastic reduces ocean pollution
  • Glass and metal can be reused
  • Every recycled item helps
How to Find the Main Idea:
  • Ask: "What is this passage mostly about?"
  • Look at the first and last sentences – main ideas are often there
  • Find what is repeated or emphasized
  • Check if details all relate to one big idea
  • Try to summarise the passage in one sentence
Warning Signs You've Found a Detail (Not the Main Idea):
  • It's and example of something bigger
  • It's very specific (dates, names, numbers)
  • Only one part of the text mentions it
  • It explains or describes something else
