Author's Purpose: Why Writers Write

Year 7 English - Reading 60 minutes Critical Analysis

Why Do Writers Write?

Every time someone writes something, they have a reason – a purpose. Understanding why a writer created a text helps you read it more carefully and think critically about what you're reading. This resource focuses on to spot the three main reasons authors write!

Exploring Three Main Purposes

Writers usually write for one of three main reasons: to inform, to persuade, or to entertain. Sometimes they combine more than one purpose!

1. TO INFORM (Teach or Explain)

The writer wants to give you facts, teach you something, or explain how something works.

  • Textbooks
  • News articles
  • Instruction manuals
  • Information websites
  • Science reports

Language clues: facts, numbers, dates, clear explanations, technical words

2. TO PERSUADE (Convince You)

The writer wants to change your mind or convince you to do something.

  • Adverts
  • Opinion articles
  • Letters asking for change
  • Speeches
  • Reviews

Language clues: "you should", opinions, strong feelings, reasons why

3. TO ENTERTAIN (Make You Enjoy)

The writer wants you to enjoy reading, feel emotions, or escape into a story.

  • Novels and short stories
  • Poems
  • Funny articles
  • Comics
  • Personal stories

Language clues: interesting characters, descriptive language, exciting events, humour

Top Tips for Finding the Author's Purpose:
  • Ask: What does the writer want me to think or do?
  • Look at the type of text it is
  • Notice the language – is it factual, persuasive, or creative?
  • Think about where you'd find this text (newspaper, book, poster?)
  • Remember: some texts have more than one purpose!
