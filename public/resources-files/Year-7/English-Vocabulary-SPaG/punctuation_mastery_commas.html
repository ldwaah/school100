Punctuation Mastery: Commas, Apostrophes & Speech Marks

Year 7 English - Vocabulary & SPaG 60 minutes Punctuation Rules

Master Essential Punctuation!

Correct punctuation makes your writing clear and professional. Commas, apostrophes, and speech marks are three of the most important punctuation marks you'll use. Master them today!

Getting Started: Commas

Commas help separate ideas and make sentences clearer. Here are the main rules:

1. In Lists

Use commas to separate items in a list (the last two items usually use "and").

Example: "I bought apples, oranges, bananas, and grapes."

2. After Introductory Words/Phrases

Use a comma after words like "However", "Firstly", "Meanwhile", or phrases that come before the main sentence.

Example: "However, I decided to go anyway."

Example: "After the match ended, we went home."

3. To Join Clauses

Use a comma before conjunctions (and, but, or, so) when joining two complete sentences.

Example: "I wanted to go out, but it was raining."

4. Around Extra Information

Use commas to separate extra information that could be removed without changing the meaning.

Example: "My friend, who lives next door, called me."

Wrong:

"I wanted to buy bread milk eggs and butter."

"After finishing my homework I went outside."

Correct:

"I wanted to buy bread, milk, eggs, and butter."

"After finishing my homework, I went outside."

Let's Practice: Apostrophes

Apostrophes have two main uses:

1. Contractions (Shortening Words)

Use and apostrophe to show where letters are missing.

Examples:

do not → don't

I am → I'm

it is → it's

we are → we're

they have → they've

2. Possession (Showing Ownership)

Use and apostrophe to show something belongs to someone.

Singular: Add 's

• "The girl's book" (the book belongs to the girl)

• "James's bike" (the bike belongs to James)

Plural ending in s: Add ' after the s

• "The girls' books" (books belong to multiple girls)

• "The teachers' cars" (cars belong to multiple teachers)

Plural not ending in s: Add 's

• "The children's toys" (toys belong to children)

Common Mistakes:
  • its vs it's: "its" = belonging to it (no apostrophe). "it's" = it is (with apostrophe)
  • your vs you're: "your" = belonging to you. "you're" = you are
  • their vs they're: "their" = belonging to them. "they're" = they are
Wrong:

"The cats toy" (should be "cat's toy" or "cats' toy")

"It's a nice day" (should be "It's a nice day")

Correct:

"The cat's toy" (one cat owns the toy)

"It's a nice day" (it is a nice day)

Going Further: Speech Marks (Inverted Commas)

Speech marks show when someone is speaking.

Rules for Speech Marks:

1. Put speech marks around what is said:

"Hello," she said.

2. Punctuation goes INSIDE the speech marks:

"Are you ready?" he asked.

"I don't know," she replied.

3. Start a new line for each new speaker:

"Where are you going?" asked Tom.

"To the shop," replied Sarah.

4. Use commas with speech tags:

"I'm tired," he said.

She whispered, "Be quiet."

Wrong:

Tom said I'm going home. (no speech marks)

"Where are you" asked Sarah. (punctuation outside speech marks)

Correct:

Tom said, "I'm going home."

"Where are you?" asked Sarah.
