Vocabulary Building: Prefixes, Suffixes & Root Words

Year 7 English - Vocabulary & SPaG 60 minutes Word Building

Build Your Vocabulary Power!

Understanding prefixes, suffixes, and root words helps you work out the meaning of new words and build a stronger vocabulary. Once you learn these building blocks, you can understand thousands of words!

Getting Started: Root Words

A root word is the basic part of a word that gives it its main meaning.

Examples:

act (to do) → action, actor, active, react

write (to form words) → writer, written, rewrite

read (to look at words) → reader, reading, readable

play (to have fun) → player, playing, replay

Understanding root words helps you:
  • Work out what new words mean
  • Spell words correctly
  • Build your vocabulary faster

Let's Practice: Prefixes

A prefix is a group of letters added to the BEGINNING of a word to change its meaning.

Common Prefixes:

un- = not → unhappy (not happy), unfair (not fair), unlock (not locked)

re- = again → rewrite (write again), replay (play again), return (turn again)

pre- = before → prefix (before), preview (view before), prevent (come before)

dis- = not/opposite → dislike (not like), disappear (not appear), disagree (not agree)

mis- = wrongly → mistake (wrong take), misbehave (behave wrongly), misunderstand (understand wrongly)

im- = not → impossible (not possible), impolite (not polite), imperfect (not perfect)

over- = too much → overeat (eat too much), overflow (flow too much), overwork (work too much)

under- = below/not enough → undercooked (not cooked enough), underground (below ground), underpaid (not paid enough)

Word Building Examples:

happy (root) → unhappy (prefix + root)

write (root) → rewrite (prefix + root)

Going Further: Suffixes

A suffix is a group of letters added to the END of a word to change its meaning or word type.

Common Suffixes:

-er = person who → teacher (person who teaches), writer (person who writes), runner (person who runs)

-ing = happening now → running, playing, reading

-ed = past tense → walked, played, finished

-ful = full of → beautiful (full of beauty), helpful (full of help), careful (full of care)

-less = without → hopeless (without hope), careless (without care), endless (without end)

-ly = in that way → quickly (in a quick way), slowly (in a slow way), happily (in a happy way)

-tion = action/process → action (act + tion), education (educate + tion), information (inform + tion)

-ness = state of being → happiness (state of being happy), kindness (state of being kind), darkness (state of being dark)

Word Building Examples:

teach (root) → teacher (root + suffix)

happy (root) → happiness (root + suffix)

Final Challenge: Combining All Three

Words can have prefixes, root words, AND suffixes!

Complex Word Building:

unhappiness = un- (prefix: not) + happy (root) + -ness (suffix: state of)

= "the state of not being happy"


rewriting = re- (prefix: again) + write (root) + -ing (suffix: happening now)

= "writing again"


disappeared = dis- (prefix: not) + appear (root) + -ed (suffix: past tense)

= "did not appear in the past"

Your Practice Tasks

Task 1: Identify Root Words
Find the root word in each of these words:

a) teacher
b) rewrite
c) unhappy
d) careless
e) slowly
Task 2: Add Prefixes
Add a prefix to each word to create a new word. State what the new word means:

a) happy (add "un-")
b) write (add "re-")
c) appear (add "dis-")
d) cook (add "over-")
e) behave (add "mis-")
Task 3: Add Suffixes
Add a suffix to create a new word. State what the new word means:

a) teach (add "-er")
b) help (add "-ful")
c) hope (add "-less")
d) quick (add "-ly")
e) kind (add "-ness")
Task 4: Break Down Words
Break down these words into prefix, root, and suffix. Explain what each part means and what the whole word means:

a) unhelpful
b) replayed
c) disappearance
d) carefully
e) misunderstanding
Task 5: Work Out Meanings
Use your knowledge of prefixes, suffixes, and root words to work out what these words mean:

a) impossible
b) preview
c) reader
d) undressed
e) kindness
Task 6: Create New Words
Create THREE new words by combining a prefix, root word, and suffix. Explain what each word means:

Example: un + happy + ness = unhappiness (the state of not being happy)

Reflection

Reflection:
How does understanding prefixes and suffixes help you understand new words? Can you think of a word you didn't know before but can now work out because you know its parts? How will you use this knowledge in your reading and writing?
