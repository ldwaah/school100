Good grammar makes your writing clear and professional. Throughout this activity, you'll develop essential grammar rules including subject-verb agreement, tenses, and how to fix common sentence errors!

Getting Started: Subject-Verb Agreement

The subject (who/what does the action) and verb (the action) must match in number (singular or plural).

Singular Subjects = Singular Verbs

Examples:

• The cat runs (not "run")

• She likes pizza (not "like")

• Tom was happy (not "were")

Plural Subjects = Plural Verbs

Examples:

• The cats run (not "runs")

• They like pizza (not "likes")

• The students were happy (not "was")

Wrong:

"The dogs was barking" (should be "were")

"My friends likes football" (should be "like")

Correct:

"The dogs were barking"

"My friends like football"

Let's Practice: Verb Tenses

Tense tells us WHEN something happened.

Present Tense (now)

• I walk / He walks

• I am happy / She is happy

Example: "I go to school every day."

Past Tense (already happened)

• I walked / He walked

• I was happy / She was happy

Example: "Yesterday, I went to the shop."

Future Tense (will happen)

• I will walk / He will walk

• I will be happy / She will be happy

Example: "Tomorrow, I will visit my friend."

Important Rules:
  • Keep tenses consistent within a sentence/paragraph
  • Don't mix past and present: "I walked to school and I see my friend" (wrong)
  • Correct: "I walked to school and I saw my friend"

Going Further: Complete Sentences vs Fragments

Complete Sentence

Must have:

  • A subject (who/what)
  • A verb (action)
  • A complete thought

Example: "The cat sat on the mat."

Sentence Fragments (incomplete):

"Because I was tired." (missing main clause)

"The big red car." (no verb)

"Running in the park." (no subject)

Fixed:

"I went to bed because I was tired."

"The big red car drove past."

"I was running in the park."

Final Challenge: Run-on Sentences

Two or more sentences joined incorrectly.

Run-on Sentences:

"I woke up I ate breakfast I went to school." (no punctuation)

"The dog barked it was loud." (comma splice - two sentences joined with comma)

Fixed Options:

Option 1 - Full stops: "I woke up. I ate breakfast. I went to school."

Option 2 - Commas with conjunctions: "I woke up, ate breakfast, and went to school."

Option 3 - Semi-colon: "The dog barked; it was loud."

Your Practice Tasks

Task 1: Subject-Verb Agreement
Choose the correct verb form:

a) The students (was/were) happy.
b) My friend (like/likes) chocolate.
c) The books (is/are) on the table.
d) She (go/goes) to school every day.
e) They (play/plays) football on Saturdays.
Task 2: Fix Tense Errors
Correct these sentences that mix tenses:

a) Yesterday I go to the shop and I buy some milk.
b) Last week we were happy but now we is sad.
c) Tomorrow I will walk to school and I see my friends.
Task 3: Fix Sentence Fragments
Turn these fragments into complete sentences:

a) Because it was raining.
b) The big red bicycle in the garage.
c) Running quickly down the street.
d) After the match finished.
Task 4: Fix Run-on Sentences
Fix these run-on sentences (use full stops, commas with conjunctions, or semi-colons):

a) I woke up I got dressed I ate breakfast.
b) The film was good it lasted three hours.
c) She loves reading she reads every night she has hundreds of books.
Task 5: Identify Errors
This paragraph has multiple grammar errors. Identify and fix them:

"The students was excited about the school trip. Last week we goes to London and we see lots of interesting things. The museums was amazing. We have fun and we is tired when we get home."
Task 6: Write Your Own
Write THREE sentences about what you did last weekend. Make sure each sentence:
• Has correct subject-verb agreement
• Uses past tense consistently
• Is a complete sentence (not a fragment or run-on)
