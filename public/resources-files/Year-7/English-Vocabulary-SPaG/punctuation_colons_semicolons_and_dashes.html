Advanced Punctuation: Colons, Semicolons & Dashes

Year 7 English - Vocabulary & SPaG 60 minutes Advanced Punctuation

Level Up Your Punctuation!

Colons, semicolons, and dashes are powerful punctuation marks that add sophistication to your writing. They help create rhythm, clarify meaning, and make your writing more interesting. Master these today!

Getting Started: Colons (:)

A colon introduces something that follows.

1. Introducing a List

Use a colon before a list of items.

Example: "I need three things: a pen, paper, and my textbook."

2. Introducing an Explanation or Example

Use a colon to introduce an explanation or example.

Example: "There's one thing I love most: reading books."

Example: "The weather was perfect: sunny and warm."

3. Time

Use a colon to separate hours and minutes.

Example: "The lesson starts at 9:30."

Important:
  • What comes BEFORE the colon must be a complete sentence
  • Don't use a colon after "like", "such as", or "for example"

Let's Practice: Semicolons (;)

A semicolon is stronger than a comma but weaker than a full stop.

1. Joining Two Related Sentences

Use a semicolon to join two complete sentences that are closely related.

Example: "The rain stopped; the sun came out."

Example: "I love reading; it helps me learn."

2. Separating Items in a Complex List

Use semicolons in lists where items already contain commas.

Example: "We visited London, England; Paris, France; and Madrid, Spain."

Remember:
  • Both sides of a semicolon must be complete sentences
  • Semicolons show that ideas are closely connected
  • You can always use a full stop instead, but a semicolon shows connection
Comparison:

Full stops: "The rain stopped. The sun came out." (two separate ideas)

Semicolon: "The rain stopped; the sun came out." (closely related - one caused the other)

Going Further: Dashes (— or -)

Dashes are versatile and add emphasis.

1. Adding Extra Information

Use dashes to add extra information (like commas or brackets).

Example: "My friend—the one who loves football—scored a goal."

Example: "The library—our favourite place—was closed."

2. Showing Interruption or Drama

Dashes can create a dramatic pause or show interruption.

Example: "I was about to leave when—CRASH!—the window broke."

Example: "Wait—where are you going?"

3. Emphasising a Point

Use a dash to emphasise what comes after.

Example: "There's only one thing I need—your help."

Dash Tips:
  • Use dashes in pairs when adding information in the middle of a sentence
  • Use a single dash at the end to add emphasis
  • Don't overuse dashes—they're powerful but should be used sparingly
