Conjunctions & Connectives: Linking Words

Year 7 English - Vocabulary & SPaG 60 minutes Linking Ideas

Link Your Ideas Together!

Conjunctions and connectives help you join ideas and make your writing flow smoothly. They show relationships between ideas - whether you're adding information, contrasting, showing cause and effect, or indicating time. Master these linking words today!

Getting Started: Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

These join two equal ideas: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So

For

Shows reason: "I went home, for I was tired."

And

Adds information: "I like reading and writing."

But

Shows contrast: "I wanted to go out, but it was raining."

Or

Shows choice: "Do you want tea or coffee?"

So

Shows result: "It was raining, so I stayed inside."

Let's Practice: Time Connectives

Show WHEN something happens.

Common Time Connectives:

First, Second, Third, Finally - for ordering

Before, After, During, While - for timing

Then, Next, Meanwhile, Later - for sequence

Eventually, Finally, Soon, Suddenly - for timing

Examples:

• "First, I did my homework. Then I watched TV."

• "While I was reading, the phone rang."

• "After lunch, we went outside."

Going Further: Adding Information

Show you're adding more ideas.

Adding Connectives:

Also, Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, As well as

Examples:

• "I love reading. Also, I enjoy writing."

• "Furthermore, reading improves your vocabulary."

• "I like football. In addition, I play basketball."

Final Challenge: Contrasting Ideas

Show differences or opposing ideas.

Contrasting Connectives:

However, Nevertheless, On the other hand, Although, Despite, Whereas

Examples:

• "I wanted to go out. However, it was raining."

• "Although I was tired, I finished my homework."

• "Despite the rain, we still went out."

Cause and Effect

Show WHY something happened or its result.

Cause and Effect Connectives:

Because, Since, As, Therefore, Consequently, As a result, For this reason, Due to

Examples:

• "I stayed inside because it was raining."

• "It was raining. Therefore, I stayed inside."

• "As a result of the rain, the match was cancelled."

Your Practice Tasks

Task 1: Join Sentences with FANBOYS
Join these pairs of sentences using the correct conjunction (and, but, or, so):

a) I wanted to go out. It was raining.
b) I like reading. I enjoy writing.
c) Do you want tea? Do you want coffee?
d) I was tired. I went to bed early.
Task 2: Add Time Connectives
Rewrite this paragraph adding time connectives to show the sequence:

"I woke up. I got dressed. I ate breakfast. I went to school. I had lessons. I came home."
Task 3: Choose the Right Connective
Choose the best connective for each sentence:

a) I wanted to go out _____ it was raining. (however/because/furthermore)
b) I like reading. _____ I enjoy writing. (therefore/also/nevertheless)
c) It was raining. _____ I stayed inside. (however/therefore/furthermore)
d) _____ I was tired, I finished my homework. (Because/Although/Therefore)
Task 4: Add Linking Words
Rewrite these short sentences, joining them with appropriate connectives to make them flow better:

a) I wanted to play football. It was raining. I stayed inside.
b) I like reading. Reading improves vocabulary. I read every day.
c) The test was hard. I studied well. I did okay.
Task 5: Write Your Own Paragraph
Write a paragraph (60-80 words) about your weekend or a recent event. Use at least FOUR different types of connectives:
• One time connective
• One adding connective (also, furthermore)
• One contrasting connective (however, although)
• One cause/effect connective (because, therefore)
Task 6: Identify Connectives
Read this paragraph and list all the connectives you can find. Say what type each one is (time, adding, contrasting, or cause/effect):

"I wanted to go to the park because it was sunny. However, I had lots of homework. First, I finished my maths. Then I did my English. Also, I had to write an essay. Although it took time, I felt good when I finished. Therefore, I was able to go out later."

Reflection

Reflection:
How do connectives improve your writing? Which type of connective do you use most? Which do you find most useful? Can you think of a time when you needed to link ideas in your writing?
