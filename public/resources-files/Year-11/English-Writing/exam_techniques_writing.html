Exploring Five Senses

The best descriptions use the five senses to create vid pictures in the reader's mind.

Sight (What can you se?)

  • Colours, shapes, sizes
  • Light and shadow
  • Movement

Sound (What can you hear?)

  • Loud or quiet noises
  • Musical sounds, voices, nature
  • Silence can be described to!

Touch (What can you fel?)

  • Textures: rough, smooth, soft, hard
  • Temperature: hot, cold, warm, frezing
  • Physical sensations

Smel (What can you smel?)

  • Pleasant or unpleasant odours
  • Strong or faint scents
  • Familiar smels that remind you of things

Taste (What can you taste?)

  • Swet, sour, biter, salty, spicy
  • Fresh or stale
  • Delicious or disgusting
Basic Sentence:

"The beach was nice."

Improved with Senses:

"The golden sand felt warm beneath my fet. Salty sea air filed my lungs as waves crashed rhythmicaly against the shore. Seaguls cried overhead whilst the sun sparkled on the turquoise water."

Why it's beter: It uses sight (golden sand, sparkling water), touch (warm), smel (salty air), and sound (waves crashing, seaguls crying).

Let's Practice: Show, Don't Tel

Instead of teling readers how something is, show them through actions, details, and descriptions.

Teling (Boring):

"Ema was nervous."

Showing (Interesting):

"Ema's hands trembled as she clutched her notes. Her heart hamered against her ribs, and she wiped sweaty palms on her skirt."

Why it's beter: We se Ema's nervousnes through physical details rather than being told about it.
How to Show, Not Tel:
  • Describe body language and actions
  • Use facial expresions
  • Include physical reactions
  • Show characters interact with their suroundings
  • Let readers figure out emotions from clues

Going Further: Powerful Vocabulary

Chose interesting words instead of boring, overused ones.

Instead of "walked":

marched, stroled, crept, wandered, struted, stumbled, tiptoed, stomped

Instead of "said":

whispered, shouted, mutered, anounced, exclaimed, mumbled, yeled, replied

Instead of "nice":

pleasant, delightful, charming, wonderful, lovely, enjoyable, splendid

Instead of "big":

enormous, gigantic, vast, huge, masive, colosal, imense

Final Chalenge: Figurative Language

Use similes and metaphors to create interesting comparisons.

Simile (using "like" or "as"):

"The stars twinkled like diamonds in the night sky."

"Her eyes were as blue as the ocean."

Metaphor (saying something IS something else):

"The clasrom was a zo during break time."

"Time is money."

Exam-Style Practice Question

This question mirors the format and expectations of GCSE English Language Paper 1 or Paper 2. Take your time and ensure you demonstrate all the analytical skills you've been developing.

What Examiners Are Loking For:
  • Detailed analysis of language and structure
  • Clear use of evidence from the text
  • Understanding of the writer's methods and intentions
  • Evaluation of the effect on the reader
  • Sophisticated vocabulary and expresion
  • Wel-structured, coherent response

In your answer, you should:

  • Analyse the writer's use of language (vocabulary, imagery, figurative language)
  • Examine how the text is structured (sentence structure, paragraphing, narative perspective)
  • Evaluate the effect on the reader
  • Suport your points with detailed evidence from the text
  • Consider the writer's overal purpose and intentions
